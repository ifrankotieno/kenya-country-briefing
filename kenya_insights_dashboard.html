Change

Why the population is still growing, and why the UN birth rate and the KNBS registered rate do not match

Two birth numbers, both real

UN-style estimates put the crude birth rate near 27 per 1,000 and fertility near 3.2 children per woman. Kenya’s civil registration report for 2024 shows a registered crude birth rate of 21.2 and registered fertility of 2.3. Registered events only count certificates that were filed. Estimates try to include births that were never registered. Use the registered rate when you mean recorded events.

Families are smaller than in the 1960s, but births still far outnumber deaths, and so many people are still young that the population keeps rising. Sources: UN World Population Prospects 2024, KNBS Vital Statistics 2024.

Learning

From literacy down to degrees, plus the 2025 KCSE snapshot

2025 KCSE snapshot

27% of candidates scored C+ or above — about 270,715 of 993,000 candidates. That grade is the usual direct university entry line. It is one exam year, not the share of all adults with a degree.

82.9% Can read and write, ages 15+, 2022
78.8% Finished primary, ages 25+
37.6% Upper secondary or more, ages 25+
6.8% Bachelor’s or more, ages 25+

Kenya gets children into school. It does not yet get most adults through secondary school, and only a small share hold a university degree. Sources: World Bank, UNESCO, KNBS.

Places

Where people and jobs concentrate

Kenya has 47 counties, but people, jobs, and services bunch in a handful of cities. National density is about 99 people per km².

Nairobi Capital. About 5 million in the wider metro area.
Mombasa Main port and second city.
Nakuru Rift Valley hub for farming and services.
Eldoret Highlands hub for education, grain, and trade.
Kisumu Lake Victoria port and main city of the west.
Garissa Service town for a large, dry northern region.

City roles are a briefing, not a census of every town. Thika sits on the edge of the Nairobi economy. Malindi is a smaller coastal centre.
